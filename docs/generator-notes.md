# 自動生成の詳細メモ（実測値つき）

`CLAUDE.md` の「ランダム生成」節から移した、**重み・確率の根拠となる実測値と経緯**。
`CLAUDE.md` には現行ルールの要約だけを置く。値を調整したら、ここに実測を追記し、`CLAUDE.md` の要約が古くなっていれば直す。
元の文面はそのまま（各項目は `CLAUDE.md` の同名項目に対応）。

---

- **The generator's search side is split by role too** — `generateOptions.ts` (`GenerateOptions`/`GenerateResult`), `generateWeights.ts` (every tunable number: `A_PRIORITY`, the `*_WEIGHT`s, `DEFAULT_MAX_*`, `preferredThrowCount`, `requiresAllElements`, `suppressHardThrow`), `generateEvaluate.ts` (what gets counted — `hardThrowCount`/`otherStyleCount`/`saltoRepeatCount`/`shapeRankTotal`/… — plus `evaluate`/`shortfallPenalty` that add them up), and `generateSearch.ts` (`greedyAttempt`/`swapIn`/`tuneAutoSeries`/`orderSeries`/`upgradeTumblings`/`satisfying`). `generate.ts` keeps `generateRoutine`/`generateForApparatus` and re-exports all four, so importers still say `from "./generate"`. The evaluation reads `-(range + over-cap)*100 - shortfall + D + difficulty premium + A - real-world preferences - tie-breakers`, and its weights come in three scales: tie-breakers 0.005〜0.05 (under the 0.1 difficulty step, so they only decide between equal-scoring routines), real-world preferences 0.1〜0.9 (these do trade against points), and requirements/range (`REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT` 10 per unmet element, ×100 out of range). Search only ever asks whether that one number went up.

- **`generate.ts`** builds a routine out of series templates: `generateRoutine(templates, {apparatus, junior, minScore, maxScore})` (and `generateForApparatus` when no apparatus is fixed) runs a randomized greedy over the usable templates (that apparatus + 共通), maximizing `-(out-of-range)*100 + dScore + aScore` — since missing required elements and junior over-throws land in the A deduction, that one number encodes "satisfy the requirements, push difficulty, stay in range" — then prunes any series whose removal doesn't lower the value, which is what keeps un-scored content (a 4th tumbling, a junior 6th throw, a duplicate series) out. 投げタン is capped at one per routine (`DEFAULT_MAX_THROW_TUMBLING`, overridable via `maxThrowTumbling`) — one satisfies the requirement, so extras carry the same penalty weight as falling outside the score range — Difficulty itself lowers a skill's pick weight (`SALTO_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT`: D 0.6, E 0.3) because the harder a single skill is, the less often it appears; with that alone a max-D routine lands on 1〜2 D-or-above singles (the level a top Japanese routine actually carries), and `HIGH_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT` (0.02) only breaks ties on top of it rather than capping anything. Ring is heavier and hard to twist while holding, so it gets fewer of those singles still: `APPARATUS_HIGH_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT` (ring 0.4, via `apparatusHighDifficultyWeight`) multiplies the pick weight and divides the evaluation weight — measured, D-or-above singles average 1.24 (stick) / 0.96 (clubs) / 0.66 (ring) with the D score essentially unchanged. ハンドスプリング and 転宙 (`LIMITED_SKILLS`) are picked with a low weight and capped at `LIMITED_SKILL_MAX` (1) per routine, counting both skill and 徒手動作 entries (`limitedSkillCounts`), and タンブリング as a whole is capped at `DEFAULT_MAX_TUMBLINGS` (= `ADOPT_COUNT`, 3, counting the 投げタン, overridable via `maxTumblings`), since only the top three are ever scored. Packing 三宙 + つなぎ + 投げタン into three lines is tight for the greedy, so when the D target makes the 必須要素 mandatory and the best attempt still misses one, `swapIn` swaps single series against the remaining candidates (bounded rounds, only on the winning attempt, and it keeps the auto-series caps) before the result is returned; if a shortfall survives that, the generator rebuilds from a forced start — `satisfying` picks the candidates that cover the missing requirement and `greedyAttempt([t])` re-runs the greedy with that series pinned (up to `REBUILD_ATTEMPTS`, 5, per candidate, each followed by the same swap repair), which is what a routine sitting exactly at the D cap needs since covering the shortfall there means dropping a series, not adding one. D is raised through difficulty rather than bonuses: `DIFFICULTY_PREFERENCE_WEIGHT` (0.3 per point of `tumblingScore + handScore`) tops up the evaluation, so between two routines with the same D the one whose points come from difficulty wins, while the premium on 0.1 of difficulty (0.03) stays under the 0.1 D step so D is never traded away for it (measured over 40 routines: difficulty 3.90 → 3.93, bonuses 0.74 → 0.69, D unchanged). **D 4.2 is exactly six E-difficulty units** (`allEScore` = `DIFF_SCORE.E` 0.7 × `ADOPT_COUNT` 3 × 2, derived from the constants rather than written down), so anything at or above it has to put all three tumbling *and* all three hand units at E **before** it can add a single bonus. `aimsAllE` reads the demanded `minScore` and turns that ordering on: `generateEvaluate` charges `allEShortfall` × `ALL_E_SHORTFALL_WEIGHT` (0.5, above a 技術加点's 0.1 and above the throw-count weights so the greedy upgrades a unit rather than buying another bonus), and `upgradeHandUnits` runs a `swapIn` restricted to throw series right after `upgradeTumblings` — the hand side needed its own pass for the same reason tumbling did: the greedy cannot see candidates that appear after its slots are full, and `tuneAutoThrows` can only stretch a chosen shape's シェネ count, which a minimal 投げ shape has no room for. Measured over 25 routines per apparatus at a 4.2 minimum, routines with **all six at E** went stick 14 → 20, clubs 12 → 24, ring 10 → 18, rope 9 → 22, with D 4.73 → 4.92 / 4.89 → 5.22 / 4.77 → 5.08 / 4.77 → 5.07; the gain is all on the hand side (adopted hand units at E 60〜67 → 72〜75 of 75) since tumbling was already 69〜74 of 75. What is left is 1〜4 units of ~75, every one of them a D sitting one step short. With no lower bound asked for, nothing changes (the pass and the term are both gated on `aimsAllE`). **Below that, the adopted units still have to be within one step of each other** (`MAX_ADOPTED_DIFF_SPREAD` 1): a real routine's scored units are {C,D} or {D,E}, never C next to E and never a B among them — the *skills* may vary freely, and a 加点-only cheap throw is untouched since it never reaches the top three. The measured cause was the 必須要素: at a 3.0 cap `requireAllElements: false` takes 幅2以上 from 12/25 to 3/25 with D unchanged, and the low unit is almost always the cheap half of a 連続投げ (`leadPair`/`trailPair`'s 投げ→1シェネ→キャッチ, the shape that buys one more 投げ方の種類 cheaply) — with 4 throws, one of them a 投げタン, there are exactly `ADOPT_COUNT` hand units so that cheap one cannot miss the cut. Three things fix it, and **an evaluation weight on the spread is not one of them** (0.5 → 1.5 → 4 all stall at 16〜22/25, and a strong one breaks other balances: the cheap throw is cheapest to remove, so 投げ4回以上 fell 85 % → 58 %, the 交互 ordering broke and `tumblingBalance` stopped responding). (1) a **demand-shaped ceiling on the adopted unit difficulty** — `unitDifficultyCeiling` is the smallest difficulty whose `DIFF_SCORE` × `ADOPT_COUNT` × 2 reaches the requested cap (2.5・3.0 → D, 3.5・4.0 → E, 1.5 → C, and 4.2 → E, agreeing with `allEScore` by construction; no cap → no ceiling), and `ADOPTED_OVER_CEILING_WEIGHT` (0.5, above the 0.32 a D→E step is worth with the preference lean, below `REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT`) charges **only what is above it** — penalising the low side is what opens the "just drop the throw" escape. (2) `levelAdoptedDiffs` runs after `upgradeHandUnits` and **walks downhill**: at the cap, thickening the cheap throw pushes D over, so another unit has to come down first and that single step never improves the evaluation — so the walk follows `adoptedSpreadCost` (how far the adopted units sit outside the best one-step window, which unlike the plain 幅 moves on every single step: {C,C,E,E} → {C,D,E,E} is 2 → 1) and keeps the most level state reached, spending at most `SPREAD_REPAIR_BUDGET` (1.0) of evaluation over `SPREAD_REPAIR_ROUNDS` (12) steps, with at most `SPREAD_REPAIR_ROOM_RUN` (2) consecutive "make headroom" steps; it never breaks the D range or a 必須要素, never lowers the throw count, and only raises it by the one throw that pushes a cheap unit out of the top three (and no further than `preferredThrowCount` + 1). Measured over 25 routines per apparatus at caps 2.5 / 3.0 / 3.5, 幅2以上 went from **25/25 in all twelve conditions** (mostly 幅3〜4, sets like `ABDE`/`ABCDE`) to stick 17/17/10, clubs 15/20/20, ring 19/17/19, rope 20/22/19, with D within 0.06 and the big spreads largely gone. **A third lever was tried and withdrawn**: thickening the cheap half of a 連続投げ to one step below the ceiling (2 シェネ at a 3.0 cap, 3 at 3.5) did take those twelve down to 8〜14/25, but it produced what the shape exists to avoid — 投げ→シェネ×3→キャッチ→投げ→シェネ×3→キャッチ, i.e. **a high-difficulty throw immediately after another one**, in 5〜22 of the consecutive pairs at the 3.5 and 3.5〜4.0 targets (0 everywhere else, since the count only rises with the ceiling), and it dropped the first-harder split at a 3.5 cap to 3/22. 連続投げ is performed to buy one more 投げ方の種類 with almost no extra operation, so the cheap half stays cheap whatever the level (`LEAD_THROW_CHENE_COUNT` 1 for `leadPair`, bare for `trailPair`) and the spread rule gives way to that. Perfect levelling is not reachable at a cap anyway — raising one unit a step needs another to drop one, and the 0.1/0.2/0.3/0.5/0.7 difficulty steps do not always cancel — and what is left is mostly one unit one step out; the remaining sources are the minimal shapes that satisfy a 必須投げ most cheaply (投げ→キャッチ / 投げ→1シェネ→キャッチ, A・B-difficulty hand units), which are deliberately not suppressed because they are the same shapes as a 加点-only throw. **An advanced routine's tumblings are almost all E**, so tumbling difficulty is preferred over hand difficulty on top of that (`TUMBLING_PREFERENCE_WEIGHT`, another 0.3 per point of `tumblingScore`) — and **which side to take the points on is the user's choice** (`tumblingBalance`, 0〜100, default `DEFAULT_TUMBLING_BALANCE` 50, a slider in `GenerateModal`): `preferenceWeights(balance)` returns the extra on each side, 0 giving the hand 0.3 and the tumbling 0, 50 reproducing today exactly, 100 doubling the tumbling to 0.6. **Its range is small and that is structural, not a weight to raise**: measured at a 3.0 cap over 30 routines, tumbling/hand goes 1.65/0.92 → 1.72/0.86 → 1.73/0.86 across 0/50/100 (1.93/1.75 → 1.96/1.71 at 4.5), because difficulty itself enters the evaluation at weight 1 so "raise the difficulty" wins whenever D is not pinned at the cap, and because 三宙・つなぎ・投げタン each need their own series, fixing tumbling at 3. Tripling or sextupling the lean only reaches 1.65/0.94 and 1.62/1.00, and at 6× the throw count starts to distort (4.0 → 4.4), so the plain 2× range stays. The lever with real range is the **series count** (`maxTumblings` 3 → 2 gives 1.25/1.37 at a 3.0 cap) but it leaves exactly one 必須要素 unmet (0.03 → 1.00, A 0.61 → 0.90), so the slider is deliberately not wired to it. D is unchanged at every setting, and since the greedy fills its `DEFAULT_MAX_TUMBLINGS` slots and never revisits them, `upgradeTumblings` runs one last `swapIn` restricted to tumbling candidates (`TUMBLING_UPGRADE_ROUNDS` 2) — that pass is what actually gets there: measured E share of tumbling units 67 % → 100 % with no D cap (tumbling difficulty 1.89 → 2.10, D 4.60 → 4.81, bonuses unchanged at 0.71) and 62 % → 87 % at a 4.5 cap, while a 2.5 cap stays at 20 % since a routine at that level genuinely does not perform E tumblings. How many throws a routine performs is shaped by the D target: `preferredThrowCount` is the mode (`THROW_COUNT_MODE_STEPS` — the rule minimum below D 2, 4 through the 2〜3 range, 5 from D 4, never below `throwCountRequired`), and `throwCountPenalty` pulls the count towards it — `THROW_COUNT_UNDER_WEIGHT` (0.1) per throw below it; above it the first extra throw costs `THROW_COUNT_OVER_WEIGHT` (0.2, or `THROW_COUNT_OVER_WEIGHT_HIGH` 0.15 from `THROW_COUNT_HIGH_SCORE` 5.0, where 6 throws get *more* common while the mode stays 5) and each further one `THROW_COUNT_FAR_OVER_WEIGHT` (0.3), because one throw over the mode is something a real routine does (a D 4-point routine may well perform 6) while two over is not (measured: D1.5 → 3 always, D2.5 → 4 at 80 %, D3.5 → 4 at 60 % with 5 at 40 %, D4.5 → 5 at 55 % with 6 at 40 %, no cap → 5 at 80 % with 6 at 20 %). These weights are above the 0.1 difficulty step on purpose: 技術加点 has no cap, so without them the greedy keeps adding throws past what a real routine performs. The rule minimum itself is never traded away — `shortfallPenalty` charges `REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT` for an unmet throw count whatever the D target, and `Evaluation.throwCountUnmet` makes the swap/rebuild repair run for it too, so a low D cap lowers difficulty rather than dropping a throw. Difficulty is only ever chased with the first four throws (the 投げタン plus `ADOPT_COUNT` hand units); beyond that a throw exists for 技術加点 alone, which is allowed to add series rather than being capped away — `DEFAULT_MAX_AUTO_THROWS` (5) sits above the difficulty-adopted count so `throwCountPenalty`, not the pool cap, decides how many throws a routine performs (measured, 3 → 5: at a 4.5 target 6 throws go from 10 % to 40 %, D 4.08 → 4.18, 技術加点 0.59 → 0.71, generation time unchanged at ~0.37 s; a cap of 8 gives results identical to 5) — and `extraThrowOperation` × `EXTRA_THROW_OPERATION_WEIGHT` (0.01) keeps 徒手操作 out of the throws that difficulty never adopts. In a series with two 投げ受け the first is usually the harder one — which is mostly a matter of *which shapes exist*: `trailPair` puts the 徒手 on the first throw and a bare 投げ→キャッチ after it (mirroring `leadPair`), and that alone moved the split from 66 % / 34 % to 83 % / 17 % first-harder, where raising the weight below only reached 71 %. Its trailing throw comes from `trailThrowStyle` (never 手以外, the second throw of a pair) and its catch from `trailCatchStyle`, drawn as a 徒手なしの投げ受け (`MINIMAL_PATTERN`) so clubs' press catch and rope's foot catch appear there too — that catch is often what ends the routine. On top of that `reversedThrowOrderCount` × `THROW_ORDER_WEIGHT` (0.005) weakly disfavours the reverse — weakly, because real routines do it (the national rope routine opens 手以外の投げ→キャッチ→視野外の投げ→シェネ→キャッチ); 投げタン units are excluded since their difficulty comes from the tumbling. Repeating the same salto across the routine costs `SALTO_VARIETY_WEIGHT` (0.02) per extra occurrence (`saltoRepeatCount`; 前宙 is exempt via `REPEATABLE_SALTOS`) — below the 0.1 difficulty step, so variety only breaks ties and never trades away real points. Whether the 必須要素 are optional or mandatory depends on the D target: `requiresAllElements` is true when no upper bound is set or it is at least `REQUIRE_ALL_ELEMENTS_MIN_SCORE` (3.0), and `requireAllElements` overrides it either way, and each unmet requirement then costs `REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT` (10) — enough to outrank any difficulty gain, but below the out-of-range penalty (×100) so the range still wins. `shortfallPenalty` is what counts them, and it also adds `A_PRIORITY` × `A_PRIORITY_WEIGHT` (0.01, below the 0.1 difficulty step) so that when they cannot all be met the ones that go are the ones a real routine gives up last: 投げの回数 = 各手具の必須投げ受け ＞ 投げタン ＞ D難度 (the `dScore` term itself) ＞ 多様な投げ受け ＞ つなぎ ＞ 三宙 ＞ つなぎの手具操作. Everything else in the evaluation is a straight D-vs-A comparison — `dScore + aScore`, same weight — so a series is taken only when the difficulty it adds beats the A deductions it brings (a 手具操作なし tumbling costs 0.2, a junior 6th throw 0.3, and so on); measured, the mandatory-elements rule costs about 0.07 of D+A on average, i.e. the comparison would usually pick the same routine anyway. Below that the shortfall is only the A deduction it already is, because a gymnast scoring 0〜1点台 genuinely does not meet them (the measured floor for a fully-compliant routine is about D 2.0). The greedy looks at the user's own templates first and fills what is missing from the auto candidates (`autoThrows.ts` / `autoTumblings.ts`), and `AUTO_SERIES_WEIGHT` (0.02, same idea as the variety weight) makes it prefer a template over an auto series on a tie. **How much of the routine may be auto-generated is the user's choice**: `autoRatio` (0〜1, a 0〜100 % slider in `GenerateModal`, default 1) is converted by `autoSeriesMax` into a count against `maxSeries` (`DEFAULT_MAX_SERIES`, 8) and enforced as a combined cap on auto series in both the greedy add (`greedyAttempt`) and the swap repair (`withinAutoLimits`); 0 builds no auto candidates at all, so the routine is assembled from the user's templates alone (measured with 3 templates: 0 % → 0 auto series and D 0.80, 25 % → 2 and D 2.51, 50 % → 4 and D 3.32, 75〜100 % → 5〜6 and D 4.04, where the per-kind caps bind first). Series order is not left to the greedy: `orderSeries` splits the chosen series into 転回系を含むもの (a tumbling unit or a 投げタン) and 投げ・徒手だけのもの and `interleave`s them so the routine alternates the way a real one does, keeping the greedy order only if the interleaved one scores worse (ordering is score-neutral except for junior's throw cap, which counts from the front). **Real competition D scores are a table, not a guess** (`COMPETITION_LEVELS` / `competitionLevelGroups`, from the owner's 2025 配布資料): 全日本 4.5〜5.0 / 3.8〜4.7 / 3.7〜4.4 for the 1〜5位・15位・30位 bands, インターハイ 3.8〜4.7 / 2.8〜3.9 / 1.9〜2.9, 全日本ジュニア 4.0〜4.2 / 3.7〜4.0 / 2.7〜3.5 / 1.7〜2.2 (all four flagged `junior`). `GenerateModal` offers it as a 大会レベル dropdown that fills the D-score range; typing in either range box clears the selection, and picking a junior meet while the scoring screen's ジュニア toggle is off shows a note rather than flipping it (that toggle is a prop, not modal state). **Scoring never reads this table.** Measured, all ten bands are reachable: 20/20 routines land inside every one, with the requirements fully met except in the two bands capped under `REQUIRE_ALL_ELEMENTS_MIN_SCORE` (インターハイ30位 0.30 and 全日本ジュニア下位 0.90 unmet elements), which is the rule working as intended. The greedy hugs the **top** of each band (4.82 inside 4.5〜5.0, 2.86 inside 1.9〜2.9), since it maximizes D within range. `GenerateModal` drives it.

- **Shapes that are physically possible but have no competition record** (a Japanese top-level gymnast might perform them) are declared in **one place**, `unseenShapes.ts` — adding one is a row in `UNSEEN_SHAPES`, carrying `chance` (a demand curve), `earns` (what the shape earns, which the evaluation cancels), `extra` (how much to dislike it on top) and `count(series)`. They are gated by demand rather than banned: `unseenChance(demand, curve)` with `UNSEEN_CHANCE_DEFAULT` is `base` (0.03) while the *demanded* D score is at or below `riseFrom` (4.5), then climbs `risePerPoint` (0.3) per point to `max` (0.4) — the mirror image of `HARD_THROW_FREE_SCORE`, which suppresses a shape *until* the demand is high. Three shapes use it: `leftHandNoViewThrow` (the **左手の視野外投げ**, an `AutoThrowStyle` flagged `rare`, stick only since 左手投げ is its 必須投げ; never dealt as a `leadPair`/`trailPair` throw), and the 通常の投げタン's `throwTumBackCatch` (**背面キャッチ**, `draws.backCatch` — dropped when a `secondThrow` follows, since 視野外で受けてそのまま投げる is impossible, and never combined with a 2つ同時キャッチ) and `throwTumLeftHandThrow` (**左手投げ**, `draws.leftHandThrow`). **Frequency is decided in two places, which is why the table exists**: the draw alone could not hold it down — each of these earns a 0.1 技術加点, so the greedy took a 3 % candidate share up to 55 % of routines — and making the evaluation weight *fall* with the demanded score produces a cliff, not a ramp (at 0.165 it is never taken, at 0.099 it always is — measured 1/20 then 13/20 across a 0.3-point change). So `generateEvaluate` charges `unseenPenalty` = Σ `count` × (`earns` + `extra`), which cancels exactly what the shape earns and hands frequency back to the draw. That cancellation is exact only where the gain is a 技術加点 tag (0.1 per tag, uncapped); where the gain is "satisfies a 必須要素" (投げタンの左手投げ) it cannot be cancelled — the requirement is worth `REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT` (10) — so that shape declares `earns: 0` with `extra: 0.1` and is held down by the plain series that satisfies the same requirement being in the pool too (the requirement cancels out in the comparison). Measured per 20 stick routines: 左手の視野外投げ 0 with no demand, 2 at a 4.5 minimum, 4 at 5.0, 13 at 5.3; the 投げタン shapes stay at 0〜3 throughout.

- **横投げ（`SIDE_THROW_TAG` = `side`）は投げ方の一種類**：ロープ以外の手具で、投げ／技の最中の投げに付けられる技術タグ（`THROW_OPTIONS_SIDE` / `canUseSideThrow`）。**多様な投げ受けの種類数には数えるが、技術加点（0.1）は付けない**（`score.ts` の `techCount` から外す）。ロープでは `apparatusBlockers`/`stripForApparatus`/`commonBlockers` が落とす。ランダム生成は `maybeSideThrow`（`autoThrows.ts`）で付ける — 手具を使ったキャッチ（押さえつけ）で受ける投げは `SIDE_THROW_PRESS_CHANCE`（クラブ **1.0＝必ず** / リング 0.6、投げタンの押さえつけは `draws.sideThrow`）。**クラブは横投げ以外の投げを押さえつけキャッチで受けない**ので、横投げにできない投げ方（視野外・手以外・二つ投げ）には `catchStyleWeight` が押さえつけキャッチを引かせず（重み0）、投げタンでは技の最中の投げが視野外なら押さえつけない、スティックの低難度（徒手 `SIDE_LEFT_HAND_MAX_MOTIONS`=1 動作以下）の左手投げは `SIDE_LEFT_HAND_CHANCE`（0.8）。二つ投げ・手以外の投げ・視野外の投げには付けない（横＋視野外は現実的でない）。

- **手以外のキャッチ × 横投げ（リング・ロープ）**：リングは首や足（後転の途中）にはめて受ける実施があり、**手以外のキャッチで受ける投げは横投げの方が多い**。`maybeSideThrow` は押さえつけ（`SIDE_THROW_PRESS_CHANCE`）＞手以外のキャッチ（`SIDE_THROW_NON_HAND_CHANCE` リング 0.7）＞スティックの低難度の左手投げの順で横投げを引く。投げタンは `TumblingDraws.nonHandCatch`（`TUMBLING_NON_HAND_CATCH_CHANCE` リング・ロープ 0.3、前転で終わる形だけ）で着地を手以外のキャッチにし、リングは `TUMBLING_NON_HAND_SIDE_CHANCE`（1＝必ず）で投げを横投げにする（技の最中の投げが視野外なら横投げにできないので手以外にしない）。ロープは横投げを持たない。二つ投げ・続く投げ・背面キャッチとは組まず、押さえつけより優先する。

- **二つ投げ→キャッチ→押さえつけてキャッチ（`splitCatch`、クラブ・リング）**：二つを違う高さに投げ、**高いほう（横投げ）**を残して低いほうを先に通常のキャッチで受け、**徒手0〜1動作**（`chene` の 0〜1 回）を挟んで高いほうをもう一方の手具で押さえつけて受ける（徒手の位置は二つとも空中にある間／2つのキャッチの間／その両方を均等に引く、`AutoThrowSpec.splitMotionAt`。両方のときは同じ動作を2か所に入れる）実施例の形（`AUTO_THROW_PATTERNS` の `twoThrowSplitCatch`）。キャッチは2つ同時ではなく1つずつ（`catchTwo` なし）で、二つ投げに押さえつけキャッチを付けない一般則（`catchStylesForThrow`）の**唯一の例外**。投げ方は二つ投げだけ（`throwStylesForPattern`）、投げは `reqTypes: twothrow` ＋ `side`、受けは `autoThrowSpecs` が専用に決める。

- **`autoThrows.ts`** — the 投げ series the generator builds itself, so templates don't have to cover every 投げ方. `AUTO_THROW_PATTERNS` holds the shapes (投げ→1〜2回シェネ→前転→転がり→キャッチ / 投げ→1〜3回シェネ→前転→キャッチ / 投げ→3〜4回シェネ→キャッチ, the last two also with a 視野外投げ→視野外キャッチ pair appended, plus 投げ→前転3回→キャッチ — no シェネ, and 縦3動作 makes it E — plus the three minimal ones, 投げ→シェネ→キャッチ / 投げ→前転→キャッチ / 投げ→キャッチ (徒手0〜1動作), which are how an apparatus's 必須投げ (左手投げ / 二つ投げ) is most often satisfied with the least operation; being cheap they show up more as the D target drops, but they are not a low-level-only thing — and two `leadPair` shapes that put a 投げ→1シェネ→キャッチ *in front* of the main throw, which buys one more throw style with almost no extra operation (a consecutive pair's first throw is usually the harder one, so the cheap lead gets one シェネ — `LEAD_THROW_CHENE_COUNT`) the way the national rope routine opens 手以外の投げ→キャッチ→視野外の投げ→シェネ→キャッチ; the lead throw style is dealt as `leadThrowStyle` (its catch is a plain one, and 二つ投げ is never the lead since it needs a 2つ同時キャッチ)). `autoThrowSpecs(apparatus)` crosses every shape with every throw style the apparatus allows (`autoThrowStyles`: 通常 / 左手投げ or 二つ投げ / 視野外 / 手以外 / その他 / 手具を使った投げ) and deals out the catch style (`autoCatchStyles`: 通常 / 視野外 / 手以外 / その他 / 手具で押さえつけて — the last one clubs・ring only — plus two catches that satisfy **two** techniques at once, weighted down to `COMBINED_CATCH_WEIGHT` (0.1) since 技術加点 pays per tag: 視野外＋手以外 on every apparatus, governed by the same `NON_HAND_CATCH_RULE` so stick and clubs only perform it off a low-difficulty throw, and 視野外＋手具を使った on clubs・ring at any difficulty, with clubs lower still through `NO_VIEW_USE_APPARATUS_WEIGHT` (0.3) since it has no competition record; measured 1 % of catches on stick, 3〜5 % on clubs, 7〜16 % on ring), and the シェネの回数 round-robin (`cycler` reshuffles each lap), so candidates repeat as little as possible. The シェネの手 is drawn weighted instead (`handsWeight`/`HANDS_PICK_WEIGHT`): **手なし first** (1), then 片手上げ = 両手上げ (0.5), その他 (0.25), 回旋 (0.1) — and since 回旋 is mainly performed over at most two motions, `cheneCountWeight` lowers its weight on a shape with more (3 motions 0.3, 4 motions 0.1; the other hands don't change with the count), so a 3〜4回シェネ shape rarely draws it (measured on stick: 手なし 45 % / 両手 22 % / 片手 21 % / その他 10 % / 回旋 3 %, and 79 % of the 回旋 candidates sit at 1〜2 motions). `catchStylesForThrow` drops 手具で押さえつけてキャッチ from a 二つ投げ candidate — both apparatus are in the air, so there is none left in hand to press with — and for the same reason **clubs cannot catch a 二つ投げ with anything but the hands**: there is no apparatus left to steady it against, so `twoThrowNonHandWeight` charges `TWO_THROW_NON_HAND_WEIGHT` (0.01 — a weight, not a ban, so the points are still reachable) while the *demanded* D score stays at or below `TWO_THROW_NON_HAND_FREE_SCORE` (5.0), the same `demandScore = minScore` reading `HARD_THROW_FREE_SCORE` uses. Ring is a hoop and can take one on the arm or neck, so it is exempt; measured on clubs 6/47 二つ投げ catches → 0/48 at a 3.0 cap and 1/45 → 0/45 at 4.5〜5.0, still 1/45 above the threshold, ring unchanged at 9〜14. The 縦3動作 shape (投げ→前転3回→キャッチ, `verticalThree`) is **a rare-ish element in its own right**, so whether it joins the candidate pool at all is one draw per generation (`VERTICAL_THREE_CHANCE` 0.25 through `rarityChance`, so the 珍しさ knob moves it) — one draw and not one per combo, because drawing per throw style leaves enough survivors that the greedy picks it anyway. It is exempt (`verticalThreeChance` returns 1) once the **demanded** D score reaches `VERTICAL_THREE_FREE_SCORE` (4.5), the same `demandScore = minScore` reading `HARD_THROW_FREE_SCORE` uses, since 縦3動作 is the only shape that reaches E on 徒手 alone; measured over 60 routines at a 4.0 minimum 11 → 3, at a 5.0 minimum 6 → 7 (unchanged), D average identical. It is normally received by pressing the other apparatus against it, so its catch style is drawn weighted (`VERTICAL_THREE_OTHER_CATCH_WEIGHT`, 0.2 for anything but 手具を使ったキャッチ) and `generate.ts` charges `VERTICAL_THREE_THROW_WEIGHT` (0.85 per series — more than the shape can add, so it only appears when the score genuinely needs it) for a 縦3動作 throw caught any other way (`verticalThreeThrowCount`) — so it only appears when the D range genuinely needs it (measured: 0〜1 per 20 routines with no range, but 9 of 10 once a 4.5 minimum makes it necessary). How readily a 手以外のキャッチ appears depends on the apparatus (`NON_HAND_CATCH_RULE`): stick performs it only off a low-difficulty throw (徒手 0〜1動作, `NON_HAND_CATCH_MAX_MOTIONS`), clubs only there and rarely (weight 0.2), ring and rope at the normal rate — A 手以外の投げ or 手具を使った投げ leaves no room for much afterwards — 徒手 0〜1動作 is what a gymnast realistically performs (`HARD_THROW_MAX_MOTIONS`), and a 転回系 in between essentially never happens — so `hardThrowCount` counts those throws followed by 2+ motions or any skill and `generate.ts` charges `HARD_THROW_WEIGHT` (0.9 per series, above the 0.7 an E hand unit can add) while the *demanded* D score stays at or below `HARD_THROW_FREE_SCORE` (5.0, via `suppressHardThrow` reading `minScore`), so the shape only appears when the range genuinely needs it (measured over 20 routines: with no demand ring 21 → 4 and rope 21 → 0 such throws, D 5.17 → 5.04 and 4.97 → 4.81; a 4.7 lower bound still produces 8 because the points are needed; above 5.0 the rule is off and it stays at 29). And since a rope routine very often finishes by catching the rope with the feet, that is one half of the **routine-ending catch** rule in `generate.ts` (`FINISH_CATCH_TAG`: clubs end by pressing the apparatus with the other one, rope by catching it with the feet; stick and ring have no fixed finish) — `finishCatchLast` moves the series ending on that catch to the end of the routine (score-neutral) and `FINISH_CATCH_WEIGHT` (0.05, under the 0.1 difficulty step so difficulty is never traded for it) makes the greedy pull such a series in when none of the chosen ones ends that way (measured over 20 routines each: reordering alone gets clubs 19/20 and rope 18/20, the weight takes them to 20/20 and 19/20 with the D average unchanged at 4.32/4.13). Measured over 25 routines each: 手以外 is 1 % of catches on stick, 1 % on clubs, 8 % on ring, 6 % on rope, and 7 of 25 rope routines end on one. Clubs and ring finish a roll — 転がり or 前転 — by pressing the apparatus with the other one, so `rollFinishShape` shapes weight every other catch down to `ROLL_FINISH_OTHER_CATCH_WEIGHT` (0.2) and a 投げタン rolling into its catch presses it with `ROLL_FINISH_PRESS_CATCH_CHANCE` (0.5, and `stripForApparatus` clears the tag on stick/rope where there is no second apparatus) — measured, 48 % of roll-finished catches on clubs and 53 % on ring are presses, the most common style on both. Continuing into a second throw out of a その他のキャッチ is unusual, so `OTHER_CATCH_BEFORE_THROW_WEIGHT` (0.2) down-weights it on the shapes that append one. **その他 is not a rare technique — it is the input that says "the rules call this the same catch, but in practice it is a different one", so it counts as another kind** toward 多様な投げ受け (`VARIETY_REQUIRED`, 3 kinds each way). The generator therefore uses it for exactly that and nothing else. `otherStyleUsage(series, r)` splits it three ways: the ones **filling a missing kind** are not disliked at all, the **spare** ones are charged `OTHER_STYLE_WEIGHT` (0.15, above the 0.1 技術加点 so decoration never pays for itself), and an その他 that is the routine's **first throw or first catch** is charged `OTHER_FIRST_WEIGHT` (1, above every real-world preference and below `REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT`) — it declares "different from the ordinary one", so it cannot come before the ordinary one exists. On top of that, `trimSpareOtherStyles` **removes the tag outright** when it fills no kind (a meaningless declaration), looping `OTHER_TRIM_PASSES` (4) times because dropping one tag turns that throw into a 通常の投げ, which adds a kind and frees the next one; the trim is skipped if it would push the routine out of the requested D range. And because it is a bookkeeping device rather than a frequency, its draw weight (`OTHER_CATCH_WEIGHT` 0.1) is held **out of the rarity transform** — raising 珍しさ must not manufacture その他. Measured per routine at rarity 0/50/100: その他 tags 0.31/0.28/0.33, of which **spare 0 and leading 0 everywhere** (before: 0.86/0.64/2.06 with 20/13/5 spare), candidate share 9.3 %/2.4 %/0.7 %, D unchanged. The catch draw is therefore always weighted rather than round-robin. `catchStyleWeight` also makes a 視野外のキャッチ off a 左手投げ rare (`LEFT_HAND_NO_VIEW_CATCH_WEIGHT`, 0.1). Two throws inside one series constrain each other, and the rule has two halves. `NO_THROW_AFTER_CATCH_TAGS` is what **no** throw can follow (手以外 — a club caught with the feet is not thrown from there; 手具を使った — a pressed apparatus cannot be thrown), dropped by `catchStylesForPattern` from the shapes that append a second 投げ受け, tag-based so the combined catches are covered too; `autoTumblings` likewise drops the 投げタン's press catch when a `secondThrow` follows (measured: the press-catch-then-throw pattern occurred 147 times in clubs candidates and 13 times per 20 generated clubs routines, 145/17 on ring — now 0 everywhere). `NO_SAME_TAG_AFTER_CATCH_TAGS` is the narrower half: **視野外 blocks only a 視野外 throw** — catching out of sight and throwing out of sight cannot both be done, but catching out of sight and then throwing in plain sight is performed (視野外投げ→1シェネ→視野外キャッチ→二つ投げ→そのままキャッチ). So a 視野外 catch stays in the pool and the *throw* draw removes the collision instead (`trailThrowStylesAfter`, since a `trailPair`'s second throw is dealt after its catch; `noViewPair`, whose appended throw is always 視野外, still filters on the catch side) — measured 178/1128 catch→throw adjacencies on stick and 226/1280 on clubs are now 視野外 catches, 5〜6 per 20 routines. `throwStylesForPattern` drops from the `leadPair` shapes, whose main throw is the *second* one, both 手以外の投げ and **二つ投げ** — a second 二つ投げ carrying much 徒手 (i.e. at high difficulty) is not performed; the realistic shape catches it right away, which is what `trailPair` builds (measured: leadPair mains that were 二つ投げ 80/400 → 0, trailPair's second throw unchanged at 105/480). The lead throw itself may be 手以外, which is how the national rope routine opens. A `rare` style (`unseenShapes.ts`) is never dealt as a lead, trail or 投げタン second throw — those are round-robins, so it would appear at a fixed rate whatever the demand. `buildAutoThrowSeries` turns a spec into a `Series` — 二つ投げ is received with `catchTwo` so `checkApparatusFlow` stays clean — and `autoThrowTemplates` wraps them as `SeriesTemplate`s flagged `auto: true` (never saved to `localStorage`). Their names are just the kind (自動生成の投げ / 自動生成のタンブリング / 自動生成の投げタン) — the series content is shown next to the name, so the name carries no description. Each candidate keeps its `spec`, so `generateRoutine` can retune it: between the greedy add and the prune, `tuneAutoThrows` walks the chosen auto series and moves each one's シェネ回数 within its pattern's `cheneCountRange` while the evaluation improves — so a D-score cap is met by shortening a series rather than dropping it whole (and a lower bound by lengthening one). `generateRoutine` appends them to the pool but keeps at most `DEFAULT_MAX_AUTO_THROWS` (5) per routine — just enough that they never crowd out the templates, while the throw count itself is left to `throwCountPenalty` — and only where they raise the evaluation, so a routine need not contain any. `autoThrows: false` turns them off (the GenerateModal checkbox, and generator tests that want a fixed pool). A user template for the apparatus is still required: with none, `generateRoutine` returns null rather than building a routine of throws alone.

- **The generator's tumbling side is split by role** — `tumblingPatterns.ts` (the shapes: salto count, つなぎ, 投げ受け, `basicLevelPattern`), `tumblingChain.ts` (連鎖のルール — what may follow what and where a chain may end: `nextSaltoOptions`/`connectOptionsAfter`/`canEndChain`/`tumblingFlowErrors`), `tumblingWeights.ts` (how often it is actually performed: `SKILL_PICK_WEIGHT`/`saltoWeights`/`rollAfterChance`/`backwardEndChance` and every 0〜1 chance), `tumblingShape.ts` (`TUMBLING_SHAPE_ORDER` and the shape ranks), and `tumblingTransitions.ts`, which **derives** a transition table from the first two — `buildTransitions(ctx)` returns `first`/`next(prev)`/`connects(prev)`/`afterConnect(connect, before)`, each edge carrying `id`, `weight`, `endNever`/`endNeedsBackwardDraw`/`endNeedsRareDraw`, `rollChance` and `throwRule` (`ok`/`never`/`kirimomiDraw`). The table is never hand-written: the editor's constraints stay a question asked of `skillOptions`, so a picker change propagates. Candidate building (`autoTumblings.ts`) does nothing but walk that table, and "why is X never generated" is answered by pulling one row — not in the candidate list (a chain rule), small weight, can't end there, or can't throw there. `__tests__/transitions.test.ts` reads the table directly instead of reasoning from a seed. `autoTumblings.ts` re-exports all of them, so importers still say `from "./autoTumblings"`. The per-candidate draws live in one `AutoTumblingSpec.draws` (`TumblingDraws`: `backwardEnd`/`rareEnd`/`layoutAfterConnect`/`backToForwardThrow`/`roll`/`pressCatch`/`twoThrow`/`secondThrow`) so `withSaltoCount` reuses the same decisions, and the "may the chain end at n saltos" test is `chainEndsOk` alone. The draw helpers (`shuffled`/`cycler`/`pickWeighted`/`pickDifferent`) are shared from `pick.ts`.

- **`autoTumblings.ts`** — the タンブリング the generator builds itself, so templates don't have to enumerate every combination of the skills a gymnast can do. Two layers decide what is valid. **The editor's input constraints**: `needsRoundoffBefore` (a 後方系 needs a ロンダート in front unless the previous skill lands backward — the builder splices one in exactly like `SeriesListEditor` does) and the picker's own `skillOptions(junior, skillFlowAfter(prev))`, which `tumblingFlowErrors(series, junior)` re-checks each built series against (asking the picker rather than copying its logic, so it follows constraint changes). **And how routines are actually built**, which is what shapes a chain: `nextSaltoOptions(prev)` takes the 系統 from the previous skill's landing (`leadsBackward` → 後方系, otherwise 前方系/側方系) and caps difficulty at the previous skill's, since a chain steps *down* (後方1回半ひねり→前方1回ひねり→前宙); テンポ宙返り/テンポひねり (`TEMPO_SKILLS`) are the exception that may go up, and are the only 後方系 a chain continues past into another 後方系 — plain 後方系 chains are rare, so they stop; a **forward** salto that lands backward (前宙半ひねり) does continue into 後方系, which is how a real 三宙 like ロンダート→後方1回半ひねり→前宙半ひねり→ダイビング前宙 is built. The other exception is a 後方伸身宙返り (`isBackLayoutSalto`, twists included): it lands backward but is normally followed by 前宙・きりもみ・きりもみ転回 (`AFTER_BACK_LAYOUT_SALTOS`, 側宙 coming after that 前宙), so that list is returned — plus, at a low weight, the 抱え込み half-twists a minority of gymnasts do continue into (`AFTER_BACK_LAYOUT_BACKWARD_SALTOS`: 後方宙返り1回半ひねり / 後方宙返り半ひねり at 0.5 against 前宙's 5, only after a 後方伸身 that actually lands backward (a whole twist — off a half-twist one you would need a ロンダート, which is the つなぎ shape instead), and only where the difficulty does not rise, since a chain steps down — 後方伸身2回ひねり→抱え込み1回半ひねり is the shape; measured 13 of 366 continuations, 3.6 %). Those land forward, so the line can go on into a 前方系 and make a 三宙. 転宙 is the opposite case: **nothing follows it but a 側宙** (`ONLY_SIDE_SALTO_AFTER`) — no other salto, no つなぎ, and no 前転 either (it is in `NO_ROLL_AFTER_SKILLS`), so a 転宙 either ends the line or leads into a 側宙. **Continuing straight into 後方系 off a backward-landing salto — no ロンダート in between — is rare-ish except after テンポ**, and that one statement needs two levers because of where the options sit. After a 後方伸身 the position also offers 前方系 (前宙・きりもみ), so `backToBackWeight` (`BACK_TO_BACK_WEIGHT` 0.3) on the edge is enough. After a **forward half twist** (前宙半ひねり) the position offers 後方系 *only*, so an edge multiplier is a no-op — and since a backward-landing salto can never be last (`canEndChain`), picking that skill at all is what commits to the shape, so `backLandingForwardWeight` (0.3) lowers the **skill's own** weight in `first` and `next` instead. Measured at the default 珍しさ: 後方系→後方系 15〜21 % of that position's continuations → 4〜6 % (6〜8 of 60 routines → 2〜3), 前方半ひねり→後方系 1〜4 of 60 routines → 0〜1, D average unchanged. Some pairs are rare-ish wherever they sit, which is what `RARE_SALTO_PAIRS`/`saltoPairWeight` is for — ダイビング前宙 lands forward so 前転・stopping there・前宙 are all available, but **前宙 specifically** is uncommon (0.3); measured 5〜7 前宙 against 4〜6 other saltos → 2〜3 against 5〜10. A **側宙 out of a 切り返し** (a backward-landing salto → forward salto → 側宙) is on the rare side, so `SWITCH_SIDE_SALTO_WEIGHT` (0.3) down-weights just that edge — which is why `next(prevId, beforePrevId?)` takes the preceding skill: `autoTumblings` keeps a `flow` array that *includes* the つなぎ技 (a connect resets which way the gymnast faces, so it must not read as a 切り返し), the same reason `rollAfterChance` reads the built items. Measured 切り返し→側宙 among all 側宙 continuations: 9/26 → 3/22 at a 3.0 target, 10/31 → 3/25 with no cap. The rest of the list is returned as-is — きりもみ系 only count as saltos inside a chain (Q&A Q7), which is exactly this position, so no difficulty ceiling applies — and it carries weights (`saltoWeights`) because the real split is 前宙 ＞ きりもみ ＞＞ きりもみ転回. きりもみ系 are not in `saltoList` at all (they only count as saltos inside a chain), so the one place they follow a **forward** salto is `AFTER_FORWARD_KIRIMOMI` (きりもみ転回 only — 前宙→きりもみ転回 is performed, e.g. 投げ→前宙→きりもみ転回→キャッチ; きりもみ is not, since landing on the neck and back cannot lead straight into a catch). The difficulty ceiling deliberately does **not** apply there — きりもみ転回's C is not "the chain stepped up" — and its `SKILL_PICK_WEIGHT` (0.3) puts it below 側宙 (1) and above 転宙 (0.2). Measured per 160 generated routines, chain-ending 側宙 98 / きりもみ転回 56 / 転宙 31; **the draw is a weak lever on that ratio** (halving きりもみ転回's weight to 0.15 only moves it 57 % → 49 % of 側宙's count, and 0.02 lands at 33), because the greedy takes a distinctive ending whenever one is in the pool — the same "frequency is decided in two places" shape as `unseenShapes.ts`, so pushing it lower means an evaluation weight, not a smaller weight here. `CHAIN_END_SKILLS` (とび前転・きりもみ — they land on the neck and back — plus きりもみ転回 and 側宙, which in practice only ever come last) end a chain: `nextSaltoOptions`/`connectOptionsAfter` return nothing after them and none of them is used as an entry or a つなぎ. 側転 is 徒手 now, so it is not a つなぎ either (it stays as the lead-in to a 側宙). `connectOptionsAfter(prev)` allows つなぎ技 only where they happen: ロンダート/側転/ハンドスプリング/とび前転 after a forward-landing salto, バク転 only after テンポ; `saltoOptionsAfterConnect` then picks from the 系統 that connect leads into, with `CONNECT_FINISH_RARE` (ただの後方宙返り) down-weighted rather than banned, and a **ハーフ (後方の半ひねり)** down-weighted by `CONNECT_FINISH_HALF_WEIGHT` (0.3) there because a ハーフ inside a つなぎ shape is more often the salto *before* the connect than after it (`isBackHalfTwistSalto` reads `twist` rather than listing ids, so lowering one cannot promote its neighbour; `basicLevel` is exempt). Measured ハーフ before/after the connect: 7/11 → 6/2 with no cap, 8/5 → 6/1 at a 3.0 target — advanced gymnasts finish a つなぎ with ダイビング前宙/後方伸身宙返り when they want a B, but a junior or a gymnast whose D sits in the 0〜1点台 does perform it. **Throwing *during* the tumbling (`throwInSkill`) is something only an advanced gymnast performs**, and the pool had to say so: the candidate counts are nearly even (8 of 18 投げタン candidates on stick) but the greedy made it the **majority** at advanced levels — measured 11 of 20 routines at a 3.0 cap and 12〜14 at 3.5 and above, against the plain throw-first shape's 6〜9 — because `chainThrowInSkill` satisfies 三宙 and 投げタン in one series and so saves a series slot, which the greedy always takes ("frequency is decided in two places" again). So whether those two shapes join the candidate pool at all is **one draw per generation** (`throwInSkillChance` through `rarityChance`, the same shape as `verticalThreeChance`; one draw and not one per pattern, since survivors get picked anyway): 0 while the requested cap is under `THROW_IN_SKILL_MIN_SCORE` (**4.0**, the owner's call — 上級者のみ) and `THROW_IN_SKILL_CHANCE` (0.4) above it, which keeps it rarer than throwing first. Measured after, per apparatus over 20 routines: **0 at every cap up to 3.5** (was 0〜8 at 2.0〜2.5 and 11〜14 at 3.0〜3.5) and 2〜6 from 4.0 up against the plain shape's 14〜18. It costs a little: unmet 必須要素 per routine went 0.00〜0.05 → 0.00〜0.20 (a 三宙 or つなぎ in 1〜4 of 20 routines at caps 3.0〜3.5) and D about −0.05, because dropping the shape pushes the generator into the tight "三宙 + つなぎ + 投げタン in three series" case more often. Lowering the threshold to 3.0 instead was measured too and costs the same (未達 0.00〜0.10, D identical), so the 4.0 boundary is a domain choice, not a performance one. **Catching during the tumbling (`catchInSkill`)** is the mirror shape, and the owner set its two knobs against the throw's: the **same level** (`catchInSkillChance` reuses `THROW_IN_SKILL_MIN_SCORE`) at a **lower frequency** (`CATCH_IN_SKILL_CHANCE` 0.2 against the throw's 0.4), drawn the same way (one draw per generation). **Only ロンダート and 前宙 may carry the generated catch** (`CATCH_IN_SKILL_SKILLS`, the owner's call) — the editor stays open to every skill, since the rules do not forbid any; this is the generator proposing only what is actually performed. That restriction is why this one shape **does not go through the transition table**: ロンダート is not a salto so it never appears in `first`, and it lands facing backward so `canEndChain` would reject it as a chain end. The shape is **投げ→その技の最中に受け→そのままタンブリングが続く**, built from a real ring routine the owner reported (投げ→ロンダート（手具を使ったキャッチ）→後方宙返り1回半ひねり→ロンダート→ダイビング前宙): the apparatus is in the air only until that first skill, so the catch sits on the **chain's first element** (the entry ロンダート, or 前宙 as the first salto) and everything after it is ordinary tumbling with the apparatus back in hand. That is why the shape **has no pattern of its own**: `autoTumblingSpecs` derives it from the plain tumbling candidates whose first skill is one of `CATCH_IN_SKILL_SKILLS` (a dozen lines after the entry draw), so the chain rules, the skill weights and the trailing 前転 all come from the candidate it was derived from — nothing about the transition table had to be rewritten, and a 投げタン candidate is never used as the source (one throw only). No `catch` item is placed, `pairAfterChance` returns 0 and `canTwoThrowTumbling` excludes it (both apparatus in the air cannot be pressed together). On clubs and ring the catch is drawn as a **手具を使ったキャッチ** (`CATCH_IN_SKILL_PRESS_CHANCE` 0.2, the owner's number) but **only when the catching skill is a ロンダート**, whose hands reach the floor; that is the **only** 受け方 the generator ever puts on a mid-skill catch (the owner's call: 視野外 and 手以外 mid-skill are not realistic, so the generator does not propose them — the editor still offers them); the throw it receives then takes 横投げ from the existing `SIDE_THROW_PRESS_CHANCE` draw, and `stripForApparatus` clears the tag on stick/rope as it does for the roll-finish press. 前宙 as the catching skill is legal but rare at this level, because catching on it means **entering forward** and `FORWARD_ENTRY_WEIGHT` suppresses that (measured, 1 of 57 ring candidates over 8 seeds). Measured per apparatus over 20 routines, before → after this restructuring: **0 at every cap up to 3.5** (the level gate), then stick 2→4, clubs 2→2, ring 1→4, rope 2→3 at a 4.0 cap, 1→3 / 0→1 / 2→4 / 2→2 at 4.5 and 2→3 / 1→2 / 1→4 / 2→1 with no cap — still the rarer of the two mid-skill shapes (29 against the mid-skill throw's 53 over the same 240 routines), with D moving 3.95〜5.00 → 3.95〜5.03 (+0.01〜0.03, since the shape now carries a real chain), unmet 必須要素 unchanged at 0.00〜0.05, press tags 0 → 0〜2 per 20 on clubs/ring and 0 elsewhere (5 of 18 such series over 120 clubs/ring routines — a little above the 0.2 draw, since a tagged catch earns a 技術加点 the greedy likes), and every candidate clean on both `checkApparatusFlow` and `tumblingFlowErrors`. A 投げタン that throws first never starts with a ロンダート — apparatus air time only allows 前方系, finished with 前転 (`THROW_ROLL_MOTION`) or 側宙/転宙/きりもみ転回 (`THROW_FINISH_SALTOS` — measured 74 % / 10 % / 17 % of 800 candidates); throwing during the last salto instead (`throwInSkill`) has no such limit, since the air time only covers what follows the throw. A chain steps down in difficulty, with one named exception: `DIFFICULTY_RISE_AFTER` lets 後方宙返り半ひねり continue into the harder 前方宙返り1回ひねり (and on into a 前宙) because gymnasts do perform that, weighted down to `DIFFICULTY_RISE_WEIGHT` (0.3) since going up is otherwise rare. Under junior rules a low D target prefers the skills junior upgrades — `withJuniorBoost` multiplies ダイビング前宙 / 後方宙返り半ひねり / 後方伸身宙返り半ひねり (`JUNIOR_SKILL_DIFFICULTY`, all C for junior) by `JUNIOR_UPGRADE_BOOST` (2) while the target is under `JUNIOR_UPGRADE_BOOST_MAX_SCORE` (3.0). **How hard a single skill may be scales with the demanded D score** (`SKILL_MAX_DIFF_STEPS` / `maxSkillDiffValue`, read from `targetScore` = `maxScore`): C up to the 2点台, D through the 3点台, the full ceiling above 4.0, and no limit when no upper bound is given. The boundaries are **inclusive from above** (`targetScore <= upTo`) because a cap is an upper bound: a routine asked for 3.0 actually lands on 2.9, i.e. a 2点台 gymnast, who does not perform a D-difficulty single. Reading it the other way put the ladder one notch out and produced exactly the "satisfy the cap most efficiently" routine — measured, a 3.0 cap carried **more** D-or-above skills than a 3.5 cap (0.57 vs 0.45 per routine) and 15 % of its routines performed only the rule-minimum 3 throws; with the boundary corrected a 3.0 cap gives 0.00 D-or-above skills, a hardest single of exactly C, and 4 throws in 85 % of routines (5 in 10 %), D unchanged at 2.92. The whole ladder is monotone again: hardest single 3.0 / 3.4 / 3.4 / 3.7 / 3.8 and D-or-above singles 0.00 / 0.45 / 0.50 / 0.68 / 0.80 across caps 3.0 / 3.5 / 4.0 / 4.5 / none. Without it the generator met a low target by **adding elements rather than lowering difficulty** — measured, an E-difficulty single appeared even at a 2.0 cap, and the average skill difficulty was flat (2.27 at a 1.5 cap vs 2.66 at 5.0, on an A=1…E=5 scale) while the salto count tripled (3.0 → 7.8). With the ladder the hardest single is C/C/C/D/D/E/E across caps 1.5〜5.0, D-or-above singles per routine go 0 / 0 / 0 / 0.38 / 0.44 / 0.76 / 0.80, and the D score still lands on target (1.47 / 1.96 / 2.46 / 2.96 / 3.46 / 4.40 / 4.81). The per-skill *average* stays shallow by design (2.27 → 2.57): a chain steps **down** in difficulty, so most skills in any routine are B/C whatever the level — what the level shows in is the hardest single and how many chains there are. On top of that, **the top difficulty of the era is almost never performed as a single**: `TOP_SINGLE_WEIGHT` (0.05) replaces `SALTO_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT`'s entry for whatever difficulty equals `maxDiff(future)` (`saltoDifficultyWeight`), since an E-difficulty *unit* is built as C→B→B rather than as one E skill — competition examples exist but are rare even at the national maximum of D 5.0〜5.1. Points-wise the greedy is indifferent (a single E and a C→B→B chain are both 0.7), so the draw governs: measured E singles per routine fell from 0.20/0.36/0.42/0.27 (caps 4.0/4.5/5.0/none) to 0.11/0.18/0.18/0.09 with the D score unchanged. Turning on 十年後モード moves that treatment up to F/G and returns E to an ordinary 0.3, which is what "the ceiling rose" means. **Three skills are harder than their rating says**, and their *frequency* is treated as one difficulty step higher (`HARDER_THAN_RATED`: 転宙, きりもみ, きりもみ転回 — the neck/back landings and the off-axis one). The difficulty **score** stays exactly as the rules have it; only two frequency reads change: `harderThanRatedWeight` multiplies the pick weight by `HARDER_THAN_RATED_WEIGHT` (0.6, the same C→D ratio `SALTO_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT` uses), applied **after** the position-specific overrides so it also reaches `AFTER_BACK_LAYOUT_SALTOS`, which is where きりもみ actually appears; and `frequencyDiffValue` feeds the D-target ceiling, so きりもみ転回 (C, counted as D) drops out of a routine capped in the 2点台 entirely. A multiplier rather than a table re-lookup, because `SALTO_DIFFICULTY_WEIGHT` only distinguishes D and above — a B→C bump would be a no-op for 転宙/きりもみ. Measured per routine (caps 2.5 / 4.5 / none): 転宙 0.22/0.22/0.24 → 0.09/0.24/0.27, きりもみ 0.18/0.31/0.42 → 0.11/0.29/0.29, きりもみ転回 0.31/0.42/0.51 → **0.00**/0.27/0.27, with the D score unchanged. Skills of the same difficulty are not performed equally often, so `SKILL_PICK_WEIGHT` multiplies the pick weight: ロンダート (1) ＞ バク転 (0.5) ＞ ハンドスプリング (0.5, and `LIMITED_SKILLS`' `RARE_PICK_WEIGHT` 0.2 on top, so 0.1 in effect — entries and つなぎ; the entry draw deals entries round-robin so a rare entry mixed into that rotation would appear once per pass no matter its weight, hence `LIMITED_SKILLS` entries are marked used and drawn by weight alone: measured, handspring entries fall from 7 % of candidates to 1.0 %, つなぎ from 4 % to 2.9 %, and routines carrying one from 25〜45 % to 0〜15 %), 前方宙返り1回ひねり ＞ 伸身前宙 ＞ きりもみ転回, 側宙 ＞ 転宙, 抱え込み = 伸身 ＞ 屈伸, 前宙 ＞ 前宙半ひねり, and テンポひねり lowest of the backward C skills except the 屈伸 ones, which stay below it (ハンドスプリング and 転宙 are already at 0.2 through `LIMITED_SKILLS`), and `CONNECT_RISE_WEIGHT` (0.3) down-weights a つなぎ finish harder than the salto before it, since a chain steps down even across a connect (E is C→B→B, not B→C→B). After a テンポひねり the next skill is テンポ宙返り ＞ any other salto ＞ バク転: `AFTER_TEMPO_TWIST_WEIGHT` (3) boosts テンポ宙返り in `saltoWeights`, and `TEMPO_CONNECT_WEIGHT` (0.3) down-weights テンポ系 as the first salto of a つなぎ candidate, since a テンポ is the only place `connectOptionsAfter` offers バク転. A salto that lands facing backward — a backward salto with a whole-number twist, or a forward one with a half twist (`endsFacingBackward`) — is not where a line stops: `canEndChain` keeps it out of the last position. Ending on the **backward** one is something a low-scoring gymnast does, so it is drawn with `backwardEndChance` — halving per point of the D target and exactly 0 from `BACKWARD_END_ZERO_SCORE` (3.0, the level where a routine starts carrying chains in several series, same threshold as `REQUIRE_ALL_ELEMENTS_MIN_SCORE`) or with no cap at all (measured: 8 % of lines at a 1.5 target, 10 % at 2.0, 0 % from 2.5 up); the draw is stored on the spec (`allowBackwardEnd`) so `withSaltoCount` tunes within the same decision. 後方宙返り半ひねり lands forward, so nothing stops a chain from stopping there, but an advanced gymnast almost always continues into a 前宙 or 側宙 — `RARE_CHAIN_END_SKILLS` with `RARE_CHAIN_END_CHANCE` (0.1, and `basicLevel` always allowed) makes the builder extend the chain by one salto instead, dropping the candidate when it cannot continue (measured: 0 of 75 advanced lines end there, 側宙 24 and 前宙 18 lead instead). Ending on a **forward half twist** (前宙半ひねり, 前方1回半ひねり) never happens in competition, so it is excluded regardless of the draw, and a 2回宙返り is exempt from all of this since it neither continues nor connects. Since nothing may follow a backward-landing salto either, those skills simply drop out of advanced chains. Throwing during a 側宙 is rarer still — practice footage with clubs exists but no competition record — so `THROW_IN_SIDE_SALTO_WEIGHT` (0.1) down-weights 側宙 as the throwing salto of a `throwInSkill` chain. **That list has to hold every skill nobody throws during**, otherwise suppressing one promotes the next: `THROW_IN_SALTO_WEIGHT`/`throwInSaltoWeight` keys both 側宙 and きりもみ転回 at 0.1, because once きりもみ転回 became a forward-salto continuation it took 21 % of `chainThrowInSkill` endings against the deliberately-suppressed 側宙's 8 % (now it is out of that pattern's top six and 側宙 is back at 9 %). **Twisting a forward salto while throwing is quite hard** — the real example is a twist-free 前宙 (ロンダート→後方伸身2回半ひねり→前宙(投げ)→前転→キャッチ) — so `throwInSaltoWeight` also returns `THROW_IN_TWIST_SALTO_WEIGHT` (0.3, above the 0.1 of the two with no record at all) for **any** forward salto carrying a twist, decided by `Skill.twist` rather than a hand list precisely so that lowering 前方宙返り1回ひねり cannot promote 伸身前宙1回ひねり (measured: the throwing salto was 前方宙返り1回ひねり in 8 of 112 mid-skill throws, now 2 of 116, with 伸身前宙1回ひねり flat at 1〜2). Note the share is **redistributed, not removed** — 側宙 went 9 % → 16 % and 転宙 8 % → 10 % of mid-skill throws, because the throwing salto is the chain's last one and `pickDifferent` will not repeat the 前宙 the chain usually already carries; making the shape itself rarer would mean dropping the candidate rather than re-drawing its last salto. The つなぎ itself sits after the first salto by default (`DEFAULT_CONNECT_AT`) but also lands **after the second** on `CONNECT_AT_SECOND_CHANCE` (0.4) when the candidate can carry three saltos, since 前向きで終わる後方系→前宙→つなぎ→宙返り is a common series; a chain never ends on the connect (`endsOk` requires `n > connectAt`, and `withSaltoCount` keeps the post-connect salto) — measured 5 of 38 connect candidates and 2 per 20 generated routines, e.g. ロンダート→後方伸身宙返り半ひねり→前宙→ロンダート→後方宙返り1回半ひねり. Finishing a つなぎ with a **single 後方伸身 salto** (2回半ひねり→ロンダート→後方伸身1回半ひねり and stop) is not what advanced gymnasts do, so `layoutOnlyAfterConnect` rejects that chain length unless `LAYOUT_AFTER_CONNECT_CHANCE` (0.1, `basicLevel` always allowed) draws it, which makes the builder continue into a 前方系 instead (`AFTER_BACK_LAYOUT_SALTOS`) — measured 3 of 46 connect candidates → 0. And a chain that ends on a **forward** salto is normally followed by a 前転: `rollAfterChance(id, prevId)` appends one at `ROLL_AFTER_FORWARD_CHANCE` (0.9) for every forward salto but 前宙, which sits at `ROLL_AFTER_FRONT_CHANCE` (0.5) since both are performed, at `ROLL_AFTER_BACK_FORWARD_LANDING_CHANCE` (0.5) after a **forward-landing backward salto** (the half-twists and ダイビング前宙 — 前転, stopping there and continuing into a 前宙 are all normal, which is also the つなぎ-with-抱え込み case), at `ROLL_AFTER_SWITCH_CHANCE` (0.2) after a **切り返し** (a forward salto out of a backward-landing one, where a roll is usually not performed — the previous skill is read from the built items, so a つなぎ in between resets the orientation), and 0 after きりもみ/きりもみ転回/とび前転 (`CHAIN_END_SKILLS` — they land on the neck and back, nothing follows) or anything `noRollAfter` covers (measured over 15 candidate passes: 13 % of 切り返し endings roll, 48 % of forward-landing backward saltos, 50 % of 前宙, 73 % of other forward saltos, 0 % of 側宙/きりもみ); the roll is a 徒手動作 only, so **the score does not change** (a tumbling unit's difficulty comes from its 転回技 alone — measured D 0.80 either way). `NO_ROLL_AFTER_SKILLS` (側宙・転宙) plus `endsFacingBackward` **and `endsChain`** drop the closing 前転 of a `rollFinish` shape — nothing physically stops a roll out of a 側宙 but nobody does it, and a skill that lands on the neck, back or head cannot be rolled out of at all (that last part was the bug behind a generated …→きりもみ→前転→キャッチ: the in-chain roll `rollAfterChance` already returned 0 there, but the 投げタン's closing roll only consulted `NO_ROLL_AFTER_SKILLS` — measured 17〜20 per 447〜465 chain-ending skills, now 0). Because that shape can still arrive by hand, from an import or from old saved data, the rule is also a **warning**: `tumblingChainEndErrors(series)` names any 技/徒手動作 sitting after a `CHAIN_END_SKILLS` skill (a キャッチ is fine — 投げ→前宙→きりもみ転回→キャッチ is the point), `tumblingFlowErrors` includes it so the generator self-check covers it, and `SeriesCard` appends it to the `checkApparatusFlow` warnings (display only, no score effect). バク転 is never used as an **entry** (`TUMBLING_ENTRIES` backward is ロンダート alone) — adding one changes neither difficulty nor any requirement (only 宙返り−A−宙返り counts as a つなぎ), so it needs a reason to be there. Among shapes that reach the **same** difficulty there is still an order of how often they are actually performed — `TUMBLING_SHAPE_ORDER` plus `readTumblingShape`/`tumblingShapeRank`/`throwTumblingShapeRank` rank them (E: C→B→B ＞ D→B = C→C ＞ C→C→B ＞ B→B→B→B ＞ anything else ＞ a single E; D: C→B = B→B→B ＞ else ＞ a single D; 投げタン E: C＋側宙 = D＋前転 ＞ a つなぎ chain throwing on the last B ＞ a plain chain throwing on the last ＞ C plus a non-側宙 salto ＞ else ＞ a single E; 投げタン D: 前方C＋前転 = 前方B＋側宙/転宙 ＞ throwing inside a C series ＞ else), and `generate.ts` subtracts `shapeRankTotal` × `SHAPE_PRIORITY_WEIGHT` (0.005 per rank, so 3 tumblings at the worst rank stay under the 0.1 difficulty step — it only decides between equal-scoring routines). **きりもみ系 cannot carry 手具操作 at all** — きりもみ/きりもみ転回 land on the neck and back, so the apparatus cannot be manipulated during them (`Skill.noApparatusOp` / `canOperateApparatus`). All three paths honour it: the editor hides the 手具操作 checkbox, scoring ignores the flag even if saved data carries it (`analyzeSeries`'s unit flags, the 手具操作加点 op count, and the 手具操作なし deductions), and `applyApparatusOps` never picks one as the salto that carries the op (measured: 76 of 82 candidate きりもみ used to carry it, now 0 of 88, with the deductions still 0 and the bonus still 0.1 per routine). Throwing *during* one is still fine — that is releasing, not manipulating (`BACK_TO_FORWARD_THROW_CHANCE`'s きりもみ 視野外投げ). 手具操作 is applied only where it pays (`applyApparatusOps`): avoiding the A deductions needs just the last salto plus the つなぎ's A-difficulty skill, so that is all a non-bonus tumbling gets; an E-difficulty chain (the only difficulty that can earn §3.5.5.5(3)) gets a second salto for the two-operation branch, and a 投げタン gets none at all — the 手具操作なし deductions only look at throw-free series — except the `throwInSkill` skill itself when the unit reaches E, which earns the bonus through `heldThrow` (measured: 46 % of skills carry 手具操作 where it used to be 100 %, with the deductions still 0 and the bonus still 0.1 per routine). Opening with a ロンダート is preferred throughout: boosting a backward first salto is what pulls a ロンダート in as the entry, and a `throwInSkill` 投げタン gets `THROW_IN_SKILL_ROUNDOFF_WEIGHT` (3) whatever the D target (measured: 87 % of candidates and 71 % of generated 投げタン lines start with one). Normal tumbling gets the same boost, decaying with the D target (`roundoffEntryWeight`: `ROUNDOFF_ENTRY_WEIGHT` 3 × `ROUNDOFF_ENTRY_DECAY` 0.7 per point, never below 1, and none at all with no cap) — that decay only removes the *extra* boost a low-scoring routine needs, where ロンダート→宙返り1本 is nearly the only shape; **keeping 前方系入り out of an advanced routine is a separate weight**, because entering forward (no ロンダート — a run-up straight into a forward salto, or a ハンドスプリング) is something only a D 0〜1点台 gymnast does: `forwardEntryWeight` multiplies every forward first salto by `FORWARD_ENTRY_WEIGHT` (0.2) once the target passes `FORWARD_ENTRY_FREE_SCORE` (2.0), and with no cap at all. **A つなぎ shape is the exception** — 前宙→ロンダート→後方系 is performed at mid level, so it is free up to `FORWARD_ENTRY_CONNECT_FREE_SCORE` (3.5). 投げタン is not touched: throwing first leaves only 前方系 anyway. Measured share of auto tumbling series entering forward, targets 3 / 4.5 / no cap: at rarity 50 30 % / 28 % / 32 % → 12 % / 8 % / 10 %, at rarity 0 14 % / 22 % / 22 % → 14 % / 0 % / 6 %, with a 1.5 target unchanged (the beginner shape stays). While a つなぎ shape under `ROUNDOFF_ENTRY_CONNECT_MAX_SCORE` (3.0) gets `ROUNDOFF_ENTRY_CONNECT_BOOST` (2) on top, since ロンダート→宙返り→つなぎ→宙返り is how a low-scoring routine satisfies the つなぎ requirement (measured share of candidates entering backward: 80 % at a 1.5〜2.0 target, 77 % at 2.5, 69〜70 % from 3.0 up, and 83 % vs 69 % for the つなぎ shapes). Nobody throws at the transition from a backward-landing salto into a forward one (`isBackToForwardThrow`), so those candidates are dropped — except きりもみ with a 視野外投げ, which is physically fine and looks good: `BACK_TO_FORWARD_THROW_CHANCE` (0.15) keeps it occasionally and `throwInSkillTypes` tags the throw 視野外. Half-twist backward saltos land forward, so 後方伸身宙返り1回半ひねり→前宙 with a throw is outside this rule and stays. A 投げタン's own throw can be a **二つ投げ** on clubs/ring (`twoThrow`, `TWO_THROW_IN_TUMBLING_CHANCE` 0.3, allowed by `canTwoThrowTumbling` only on the shapes that throw *before* the saltos — the editor does allow a 二つ投げ mid-salto, but the generator does not propose one): the series becomes 二つ投げ→タンブリング→2つ同時キャッチ, which satisfies the apparatus's 必須投げ and 転回系の投げ受け in one series; both apparatus are in the air, so the press catch is dropped and no skill in between carries 手具操作 (measured: 23 % of clubs and 19 % of ring 投げタン candidates, `checkApparatusFlow` clean, but only 0〜6 per 25 generated routines since a cheap 二つ投げ series satisfies the requirement just as well). A 投げタン can also be followed by a second 投げ受け inside the same series (`secondThrow`, dealt from `secondThrowStyles` — 手以外 is never the second throw, while stick's 左手投げ and clubs/ring's 二つ投げ are fine since the apparatus is back in hand); throwing before the salto makes that continuation more likely than throwing during it (`PAIR_AFTER_THROW_FIRST_CHANCE` 0.5 vs `PAIR_AFTER_THROW_IN_SKILL_CHANCE` 0.2, via `pairAfterChance`). `AUTO_TUMBLING_PATTERNS` holds the six shapes (連続1〜3本 / つなぎ技入り / 投げ受け＋前転 / 投げ受け＋側宙 / `connectThrowInSkill`: a つなぎ shape whose post-connect salto carries the throw (`connectAt` is `count - 1` for a `throwInSkill` pattern so the throw lands right after the connect, and the throwing salto is almost always ダイビング前宙 or 前宙 — `THROW_AFTER_CONNECT_SALTOS` × `THROW_AFTER_CONNECT_WEIGHT` (5) in `afterConnect`; measured 60 % ダイビング前宙 and 15 % 前宙 across 439 candidates, 1 of 20 stick and 7 of 20 clubs routines carry one). Because a connect resets which way the gymnast is facing, both the throw-position check and the 視野外 tagging read the **preceding item** rather than the previous salto (`chainEndsOk`'s `connectId` argument, `throwInSkillTypes(prevSkillId(items, …))`) — otherwise a ロンダート→…→ロンダート→前宙(投げ) would be rejected as a 切り返しの投げ / `throwInSkill`: a 2〜3-salto chain whose **last** salto carries the throw, finished 前転→キャッチ — the air time only constrains what comes after the throw, so the entry ロンダート and the chain in front are free, and one series then satisfies both 三宙 and 投げタン, which is how the national clubs routine does it: ロンダート→後方伸身2回半ひねり→前宙(投げ)→前転→キャッチ). **The vocabulary is the user's**: `generateRoutine` passes `usedSkillIds(own templates)` as `skillIds`, so the chains only combine skills the templates already show (a shape with no usable skill is skipped, not invented); with no templates at all the full skill list is used, which is what lets a routine be generated from nothing. Candidates carry their `spec` like the throws, so `tuneAutoSeries` can raise or lower a chain's 宙返りの本数 (`saltoCountRange` / `withSaltoCount`) to meet a D-score range, and at most `DEFAULT_MAX_AUTO_TUMBLINGS` (4 — three for the difficulty top-3 plus one that may carry a required element) go into a routine. A D target below `BASIC_LEVEL_MAX_SCORE` (2.0 — a gymnast scoring 0〜1点台) switches the candidates to `basicLevel`: what those routines actually look like is ロンダート→宙返り1本で終わり, no 三宙 (`BASIC_LEVEL_MAX_SALTOS` = 2), no つなぎ, and nothing above C (`BASIC_LEVEL_MAX_DIFF`), so the generator stops proposing a 2回半ひねり just because the total lands in range. `autoTumblings: false` turns them off.

- **The per-skill pick weights are the user's to edit** (`skillWeights.ts` + `SkillWeightModal`, opened from `GenerateModal`). The measured tables (`SKILL_PICK_WEIGHT` and friends) are never rewritten — the store holds only a **multiplier per skill that differs from the default 1**, kept in `localStorage` under `mens-rg-scorer:skill-weights:v1`, so 全体リセット is "throw the store away" and 1技だけ戻す is "delete that key" (`resetSkillWeights`/`resetSkillWeight`, and `setSkillWeight` drops a key that is set back to 1). `normalizeSkillWeights` discards unknown ids, non-numbers and out-of-range values (clamped to `SKILL_WEIGHT_MIN` 0 〜 `SKILL_WEIGHT_MAX` 3, `SKILL_WEIGHT_STEP` 0.1) so a corrupt store never breaks startup. It rides through `GenerateOptions.skillWeights` → `autoTumblingSpecs` → `TransitionContext`, multiplying in `baseSkillWeights` **and again after the position-specific overrides** (`AFTER_BACK_LAYOUT_SALTOS`, `CONNECT_FINISH_RARE`, the テンポ row) so an edit is not silently erased where those tables replace the weight, plus the entry draw (`entryWeight`) — and a skill set to **0 is dropped from the candidate pool entirely** (`usableSkills`). It changes generation only: **scoring never reads it**.

- **How unusual the generated series are is the user's choice** (`GenerateOptions.rarity`, 0〜100, default `DEFAULT_RARITY` 50, a slider in `GenerateModal`). Every edge weight in the transition table already *is* "how often this is performed", so the knob is one monotone transform of those weights: `rarityExponent` maps 0 → `RARITY_COMMON_EXPONENT` (3, sharpening onto the most likely), 50 → 1 (exactly the measured frequencies) and 100 → `RARITY_RARE_EXPONENT` (−1, inverting them). `pickWeighted`/`pickDifferent` take that exponent (`rarityWeight`, and a weight of 0 stays unpickable even inverted), and the 0〜1 draws go through `rarityChance(p, rarity)` = `p^k / (p^k + (1-p)^k)`, which leaves 0.5, 0 and 1 alone and moves everything else the same direction — so a "rare series" means the rare *shapes* too (二つ投げ mid-tumbling, the きりもみ 視野外投げ, the つなぎ-after-second position, the unseen shapes), not just rare skills. `pick.ts` owns it since both `autoThrows.ts` and `autoTumblings.ts` draw through it; `cycler` round-robins are deliberately untouched (they exist to spread candidates evenly, not to model frequency). **The evaluation is not touched**, so a shape whose difficulty moves the score barely responds to the knob — what responds is everything score-neutral. Measured over 36 routines per setting, rarity 0/25/50/75/100: rare skills (ハンドスプリング・転宙・きりもみ系・テンポひねり) per routine 0.06 / 0.47 / 1.08 / 2.28 / 2.89, distinct skills used 17 / 22 / 24 / 25 / 24, D score 5.05 / 4.99 / 4.98 / 5.14 / 5.27. その他 is deliberately exempt (see below): it is a variety-bookkeeping input, not a frequency, so the knob neither manufactures nor suppresses it.

## 二つ投げのシリーズは1構成に1本まで（2026-10-05）

**経緯**：クラブ・リング20構成ずつの実測で、二つ投げのシリーズが投げと投げタンの**両方**に出る構成が 10〜25 %、投げのシリーズだけで1構成に1本を超える条件もあった（クラブ上限なし 20構成に24本）。二つ投げは必須投げで1回やれば足り、投げ方の種類にも数えず、投げタンに載せても加点にならない。

**実装**：評価に `extraTwoThrowSeriesCount` × `TWO_THROW_EXTRA_WEIGHT`（1。現実志向の重み 0.1〜0.9 より上、`REQUIRED_ELEMENT_WEIGHT` 10 より下）。重みだけでは足りず、2つの穴を塞いだ：
1. 貪欲法は足す・外すしかしないので、同点で二つ投げでない投げに替える手がない → `dropExtraTwoThrow`（二つ投げでない候補への `swapIn`。**シェネ回数違いの別案も候補にしないと**、Dスコアの上限を踏み越えて替えられない）。
2. `levelAdoptedDiffs` は予算 `SPREAD_REPAIR_BUDGET`（1.0）まで評価を落とす手を採るので、重み1の罰は**払えてしまう**（段階ごとの本数を測ると、均しの段で2本目が入っていた）→ その手を明示的に弾く。

**優先度**：投げタンの二つ投げは `twoThrowTumblingCount` × `TWO_THROW_TUMBLING_WEIGHT`（0.3）で通常の二つ投げより下げる。禁止にしないのは、必須投げと転回系の投げ受けを1本で満たせる、Dスコアの上限が低い帯の効率的な形だから。

**実測**（クラブ・リング 上限3.0 / 4.5 / なし × 各20構成）：二つ投げのシリーズは**全120構成で1本、両方は 0**（前：両方 2〜5/20）。投げタンで実施する割合は 26/120 → 17/120（上限3.0 で 8/20 → 5〜6/20、4.5 以上は 1〜2/20）。D は 4.088 → 4.097、1構成あたりの技数 10.61 → 10.66 で変わらず（96→360構成、手具4つ × 上限3.0 / 4.5 / なし）。

## リングの二つ投げを横投げにする（2026-10-06）

**経緯**：二つ投げに横投げが付くのは `splitCatch`（二つ投げ→キャッチ→押さえつけ）の高いほうだけで、2つ同時キャッチで受ける通常の二つ投げ・投げタンの二つ投げ・技の最中の二つ投げには付けていなかった。リングは二つ投げも横に投げる実施があり、**二つ投げ＋横投げ＋2つ同時キャッチ＋手以外のキャッチ**もある。頻度は通常の横投げより少し低い程度で、珍しくはない（オーナー指定）。

**実装**（リングだけ。クラブは付けない）：
- 通常の二つ投げ：`maybeSideThrow` が `SIDE_THROW_TWO_THROW_CHANCE`（0.5）。手以外のキャッチで受けるときは `SIDE_THROW_TWO_THROW_NON_HAND_CHANCE`（0.6）。視野外・手以外の投げには付けない。
- 投げタンの二つ投げ：`draws.twoThrowSide`（同じ確率）。さらに**手以外のキャッチを許す**（二つ投げの投げタンは従来 `!draws.twoThrow` で手以外のキャッチを引かなかった。リングは横投げが前提の `nonHandSide` のときだけ許し、クラブは受けられないので引かない）。
- 引かない手具（クラブなど）では乱数を使わない（他の手具の生成が動かないように）。
- 技の最中の二つ投げは、生成がそもそも作らない（`canTwoThrowTumbling` は投げを宙返りの前に置く形だけ）ので対象外。

**実測（候補の段階、リング 40 seed）**：
- 二つ投げの通常の投げ 541 本のうち、横投げ 259（53 %）。手以外のキャッチで受ける 142 本のうち横投げは 49 % → 62 %。
- 投げタンの二つ投げ 138 本のうち、横投げ 49（37 %。候補は本数違いを含み相関が強いので 50 % からずれる）。手以外のキャッチで受ける形は 0 本 → 7 本（すべて横投げ）。
- クラブは横投げ 0（二つ投げ→押さえつけを除く）。
- 生成された構成（60 構成、上限 3.0 / 4.5 / なし）：リングで二つ投げが横投げの構成は 28/60（47 %）、クラブは 5/60（二つ投げ→押さえつけの形だけ）。

## 2つとも空中にある2つの投げ（`airPair`、2026-10-09）

**形**：投げ → 徒手 → **横投げ** → 徒手 → キャッチ（通常）→（転がり）→ 手具を使ったキャッチ。クラブ・リング。`overlap`（投げ→徒手→投げ→キャッチ→徒手→キャッチ）との違いは、2つ目を投げたあとも**2つとも空中にある**まま徒手をすること。キャッチの間に入れてよい徒手は転がりだけ。リングは最後の受けが手以外のキャッチでもよい（横投げが前提で、2つ目が横投げなので組める。手具を使ったキャッチとは排他）。

**オーナーの指定**：動作は片方だけ高難度（3〜4）でもう一方は 0〜1、1つ目は通常の投げか背面、頻度は通常の投げ受けより低め。

**実装**：パターン2つ（`airPairFirstHigh`／`airPairSecondHigh`、`chene` 3〜4）× 通常の投げ1つ。`AutoThrowSpec.firstThrowStyle`（通常／視野外）、`airLowCount`（0／1）、`airRoll`（転がり）。候補に出すのは呼び出しごとに1回の抽選 `AIR_PAIR_PATTERN_CHANCE`（0.2。`overlap` は 0.3）。

**実測**（候補、200 seed）：クラブ・リングとも 25/200 呼び出しに出る（抽選 0.2 に対して低めに出たのは乱数の揺れ。1回に2形）。リング 50 形のうち最後の受けが手以外 22・手具を使った 28、転がりありは約半分。1つ目は通常と背面がほぼ半々。スティック・ロープは 0。手具の流れの警告（`checkApparatusFlow`）は 0。
